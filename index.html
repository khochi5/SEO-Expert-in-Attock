  • Remove what is unused — inactive themes and plugins that are installed "just in case" deleted, so they stop slowing the site and stop being a security risk
  • Keep everything updated — WordPress, the theme and the remaining plugins kept on current versions, with updates handled carefully so nothing breaks

The practical speed fixes that make the biggest difference on a typical WordPress site.

  • Image sizes under control — images uploaded at sensible sizes and compressed, instead of full-size originals that take seconds to load
  • Caching basics — page caching set up so repeat pages load quickly, configured to suit your hosting rather than copied from a tutorial
  • Not overloading pages — heavy sliders, pop-ups and extras that add weight without helping visitors reviewed and trimmed where they are hurting load times

Categories and tags used the way they were meant to be — as organisation, not duplication.

  • Categories used sensibly — a small, clear set of categories that group your content logically, so visitors and Google can follow the site's shape
  • No tag-page duplication — stopping dozens of near-empty tag pages from flooding Google's index and diluting the pages that actually matter

Media optimization

Images that load fast and tell Google what they show.

  • Descriptive alt text — every meaningful image given a short, honest description, so Google understands it and screen readers can read it aloud
  • Compressed images — image files reduced to a sensible weight before and after upload, keeping pages fast without visible quality loss
Watch out

Common WordPress SEO mistakes

These are the problems found again and again on WordPress websites. Most are quick to fix once someone actually looks for them.

The "discourage search engines" box is still ticked

WordPress has a setting that asks search engines not to index the site. It is often switched on while a site is being built — and then forgotten. While it is on, Google stays away, however good the website is.

Demo and sample pages are still indexed

The "Hello world" post, sample pages and placeholder text that came with the theme are still live and in Google's index — telling Google the site is unfinished and diluting the real pages.

Dozens of plugins are slowing the site down

A plugin added for every small job, many of them unused or overlapping. Each one adds weight and code to every page, and together they can make an otherwise good website painfully slow.

Images are uploaded huge and uncompressed

Photos straight from a phone or camera, several megabytes each, placed on pages that then take seconds to open — especially for visitors on mobile connections.

Every post is stuffed into many categories and tags

One post filed under five categories and a dozen tags creates a crowd of near-duplicate, thin listing pages — and Google spends its attention on those instead of your real content.

Posts and service pages are never linked to each other

Blog posts sit in isolation and service pages receive no links from the rest of the site. Without internal links, Google — and visitors — cannot tell which pages matter most.
